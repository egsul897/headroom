### C.1 — Row I-D1 (Indenture, Fixed + Incurrence-Based concurrency, debt)

**Status: VERIFIED.** Document: Indenture. Section: **§13.1(a)** (Article XIII, "Measuring Compliance," captioned "Compliance in Connection with Certain Investments and Repayments" — the caption is generic, but the operative text is the concurrency/reclassification mechanic).

Source language (confirmed this session, quoted in full): *"[W]ith respect to any amounts Incurred or transactions entered into (or consummated) in reliance on a provision of any covenant that does not require compliance with a financial ratio or test (any such amounts, the 'Fixed Amounts') substantially concurrently or in a series of related transactions with any amounts Incurred or transactions entered into (or consummated) in reliance on a provision in such covenant that requires compliance with any such financial ratio or test, including the Consolidated Senior Secured Net Leverage Ratio, Consolidated Total Net Leverage Ratio, or the Fixed Charge Coverage Ratio (any such amounts, the 'Incurrence Based Amounts' and, any such basket relating to such financial ratio or test, the 'Incurrence Based Baskets'), it is understood and agreed that the Fixed Amounts (and any cash proceeds thereof) in such covenant shall be disregarded in the calculation of the financial ratio or test applicable to the Incurrence Based Amounts in such covenant in connection with such incurrence, but full pro forma effect shall be given to all applicable and related transactions..."*

Conditions: (1) the Fixed-Amount basket and the Incurrence-Based basket must be used "substantially concurrently or in a series of related transactions"; (2) the Fixed Amount must itself independently qualify under its own basket's terms. Directionality: one-way — the Fixed Amount's dollars are disregarded *from the Incurrence-Based test's ratio calculation*; the reverse (Incurrence-Based dollars disregarded from a Fixed test) is not stated and a Fixed basket has no ratio test to disregard anything from. Not symmetric. State/history: no — a point-in-time concurrency test, not a usage-history test. Timing: yes — "substantially concurrently" is itself a timing condition. Affects: ratio calculation (denominator composition) and, derivatively, capacity. Confidence: **High** — verbatim text, directly on point. Notes: the task's proposed row correctly warns not to infer this applies to *every* fixed/ratio pair — §13.1(a) is a *general* mechanic that applies whenever the "substantially concurrent" and "own qualification" conditions are met; it is not itself a list of which specific baskets pair with which. **One important refinement not in the supplied row**: §13.1(a) also states *"each amount Incurred or transaction entered into (or consummated) will be deemed to have been Incurred, entered into, or consummated, first, to the extent available, pursuant to the relevant Incurrence Based Basket"* — i.e., there is a **default ordering** (Incurrence-Based basket absorbs first) that a naive "disregard" framing alone does not capture. This is carried into the corrected table (§D) as an explicit note on Row I-D1.

### C.2 (addendum item C) — Does clause (6) usage draw down clause (24)/(25) capacity? **RESOLVED: No — independent, combinable pools.**

Source language directly on point, confirmed this session, from the Permitted Liens definition's closing paragraph: *"(x) a Lien need not be Incurred solely by reference to one basket of Permitted Liens described in this definition but may be Incurred under any combination of such baskets (including in part under one such basket and in part under any other such basket), (y) in the event that a Lien (or any portion thereof) meets the criteria of one or more of such baskets of Permitted Liens, the Company shall, in its sole discretion, classify or reclassify, or at any later time divide, classify or reclassify, such Lien (or any portion thereof) in any manner that complies with this definition, and (z) in the event that a portion of the Indebtedness secured by a Lien could be classified as secured in part pursuant to clause (6) or (24) of this definition (giving effect to the Incurrence of such portion of such Indebtedness), the Company, in its sole discretion, may classify such portion of such Indebtedness (and any Obligations in respect thereof) as having been secured pursuant to clause (6) or (24) of this definition and thereafter the remainder of the Indebtedness as having been secured pursuant to one or more of the other clauses of this definition."*

**Reading:** clauses (x) and (y) establish that every Permitted Liens basket — including (6), (24), and (25) — is independently available and freely combinable; there is no netting mechanic anywhere in the definition that reduces clause (24)/(25) headroom because clause (6) was used, or vice versa. Clause (z) addresses a narrower, different question: what happens when the *same portion of the same debt* could be classified under **either** clause (6) or clause (24) — the Company elects which clause governs *that portion*, and the *remainder* is then tested under whichever other clause applies. This is a **classification election for overlapping eligibility**, not a capacity-netting rule. It exists precisely because clause (6) requires no ratio test while clause (24) does — the Company would always prefer to classify overlapping debt under clause (6) (no ratio test) rather than "spend" clause (24)'s ratio-tested capacity on debt that didn't need it, and clause (z) confirms that election is available. Nothing in clause (z) or elsewhere states that using clause (6) for a given dollar reduces the aggregate amount of *other, non-overlapping* debt that can separately qualify under clause (24) or (25).

**Conclusion: clause (6) and clauses (24)/(25) are independent, additive pools.** Debt that is eligible under clause (6) (i.e., sourced from §3.3(b)(i) or (b)(iv)) does not need to fit inside clause (24) or (25) at all, and its use does not reduce clause (24)/(25)'s own availability for *other* debt (e.g., debt sourced from the general debt basket, §3.3(b)(xii), which is not clause-(6)-eligible and must independently qualify under (24) and/or (25)). This is the addendum's single largest flagged open question, and it resolves in the direction that **materially increases** total secured-lien capacity relative to any methodology that netted the two together — consistent with, and further strengthening, the "true figure is at least as large as, and very likely larger than, $4,041M" finding already flagged in `docs/coherent-interaction-matrix-v1.md` §D/§G. **No new numeric maximum is computed here**, per the task's hard rule — this is a methodology finding, not a recalculation.

## O. Reclassification mechanics

Two textually distinct mechanics exist, and must not be conflated in the solver graph:

1. **Indenture §13.1(a) / Credit Agreement §1.07(b) — Fixed → Incurrence-Based, silent/automatic, cross-basket.** Applies to *any* Fixed Amount basket whenever the corresponding Incurrence-Based test would, at any later time, independently permit the amount. No borrower election or notice is required; the reclassification is automatic and continuous ("at any time"). Modeled as a `RuleActivationCondition` with `effect: PARAMETER_VALUE` is not quite right either — this is better modeled as a state-transition rule evaluated at each measurement date against `historicalUsage`, consistent with `docs/solver-architecture-design.md` §E.5's treatment ("modeled as a borrower election among qualifying elections... a reclassification is simply the borrower's chosen election").
2. **Credit Agreement Incremental Amount definition, proviso (C) — Cash-Capped → Ratio-Based, opt-out, single-facility-internal only.** Narrower in scope (applies only among the three Incremental Amount components, not across all of §6.01's categories) and opt-out rather than silent (the Borrower may provide written notice *not* to redesignate). This is a genuinely different mechanic from (1) and should be modeled as its own state-transition rule with an `optOutAvailable: true` flag, not merged into (1)'s generic shape.

Both mechanics only ever move capacity *toward* an Incurrence-Based/Ratio-Based category, never the reverse — no "downgrade" mechanic (Incurrence-Based → Fixed) was found in either document.
