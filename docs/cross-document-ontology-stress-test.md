# Cross-Document Ontology Stress Test

**Purpose**: The Coherent-derived legal ontology (`docs/legal-model-remediation-design.md`, `docs/coherent-interaction-matrix-v1.md`, `docs/coherent-credit-agreement-amendment-reconstruction.md`) was built entirely from one company's documents. This report tests whether that ontology generalizes to four real, materially different negotiated capital structures, using only their actual filed governing documents fetched from SEC EDGAR in this session. It is read-only analysis: no schema, engine, seed data, or test files were touched to produce it.

---

## A. Executive conclusion

**(ii) The current ontology is materially incomplete and needs new core primitives.**

Most of the Coherent-derived *permission-level* vocabulary — a permission's grant type (debt/lien), its amount kind (fixed vs. incurrence-based), the six-way stacking-relationship taxonomy, best-path-wins OR semantics, borrower reclassification elections, and a measurement basis broader than "currently outstanding" — reproduced cleanly across all four comparison companies with only parameter changes (different dollar figures, different ratio thresholds, different section numbers). That part of the design is not an artifact of Coherent's drafting; it is standard leveraged-finance vocabulary, independently confirmed in real, unrelated documents. But four *structural* gaps showed up that no amount of parameterization closes: (1) a company's debt-incurrence baskets are sometimes defined by direct reference to another document's outstanding balance (TransDigm's credit agreement caps how much may be outstanding under its own Notes indentures; Community Health Systems' notes indenture pools its credit-facility basket with specific named note series under one shared dollar cap) — this makes "MIN across independently-computed document capacities" provably wrong in two of four real companies tested, not merely incomplete; (2) an asset-based revolving facility's own capacity is computed from external, periodically-certified collateral values (eligible receivables/inventory advance rates, net orderly liquidation value, lender-discretionary reserves) that the existing `REF`/`SUM`/`MIN`/`MAX` tree over static `CovenantProvision` rows cannot express at all, confirmed in two real ABL facilities; (3) lien priority is scoped to a specific collateral pool, not a single per-permission secured/unsecured flag — the same lien permission is first-priority on one asset pool and second-priority on another, simultaneously, in two real companies; (4) covenant applicability itself is dynamic in ways Coherent never exposed — an entire covenant package can switch off on an investment-grade rating trigger and switch back on later (TransDigm), and a single covenant can spring on/off based on a liquidity state rather than a threshold changing value (Petco, CommScope/Vistance) — both are state-transition mechanics structurally different from Coherent's step-up/cool-down (which changes a threshold's *value*, never whether it applies at all).

## D. Candidate ontology before testing

Restated from `legal-model-remediation-design.md` §3 and `coherent-interaction-matrix-v1.md` §I, as a fixed baseline:

1. **Permission** — a contractual grant with `grants: DEBT_INCURRENCE | LIEN`, `amountKind: FIXED | INCURRENCE_BASED`, and `measurementBasis`.
2. **StackingRelationshipType** — pairwise relationship between two permissions: `CONCURRENT_DISREGARDED`, `CONCURRENT_COUNTED`, `ALTERNATIVE`, `MUTUALLY_EXCLUSIVE`, `INDEPENDENT`, `UNKNOWN_REVIEW_REQUIRED`.
3. **Independent debt/lien constraint systems**, combined via `min(debtSideMax, lienSideMax)` unless an explicit cross-side relationship says otherwise.
4. **TransactionAssumptions** (rate, EBITDA adjustment, funding source, concurrent repayment, transaction date) vs. **FinancialFieldProvenance** (`REPORTED_GAAP` / `COVENANT_DEFINED_RECONCILIATION` / `RECONSTRUCTED_ESTIMATE` / `TRANSACTION_ASSUMPTION`, each with `UNVERIFIED`/`VERIFIED`/`DISPUTED`).
5. **Covenant applicability / contractual path** — whether a given financial covenant conditions a specific transaction path is itself declared data, not assumed universal.
6. **MeasurementBasis** — `CUMULATIVE_INCURRED` / `CURRENTLY_OUTSTANDING` / `NET_OF_REPAYMENT`, plus a fourth basis discovered in Coherent's own CA: prepayment-history-based (the Prepayment-Based Incremental Facility).
7. **`ALT` node / best-path-wins** — status = modeled if at least one alternative is modeled; only falls to worst status if zero members are modeled.
8. **Automatic/unconditional lien permission tied to a specific debt basket** — a lien permission with no capacity of its own, parasitic on a named debt basket's own sizing (Coherent I-6/C-7), explicitly distinguished from `CONCURRENT_DISREGARDED`.
9. **Ordered priority list among 3+ stackable permissions** — a total order over a group (Coherent's Ratio-Based → Prepayment-Based → Cash-Capped), distinct from a pairwise relationship.
10. **Leverage-neutral alternative test shape** — "ratio ≤ threshold OR ratio ≤ pre-transaction level," framed as a specific instance of `ALT`, not a new primitive.
11. **Step-down schedule as a first-class threshold shape** — several (date-range, threshold) pairs within one un-amended clause, distinct from amendment-style `effectiveFrom`/`effectiveTo` row versioning, flagged but not resolved.
12. **`modelingStatus: KNOWN_NOT_MODELED`** — distinguishes "we know this exists and haven't entered it" from `not_tested`'s "nothing recorded."
13. **Financial Covenant Step-Up / Cool-Down** — a temporary threshold increase triggered by a Material Acquisition, with a minimum-consecutive-quarters cooldown gate before it can retrigger (discovered in Coherent's Amendment No. 4, not in the original interaction matrix).
14. **Automatic redesignation (opt-out) between Incremental Facility components** — Cash-Capped-sourced debt automatically redesignates as Ratio-Based-sourced when the Ratio-Based test would permit it, unless the borrower objects (Coherent Amendment No. 4).
15. **Reallocated Amount** — unused capacity in one basket (General Debt Basket) automatically feeds an additive component of another basket (Cash-Capped Incremental) (Coherent Amendment No. 4).
16. **Priority Debt / Collateral Suspension Period** — a new, only-partially-investigated Coherent concept (dollar-for-dollar netting against several other baskets during a suspension period), explicitly flagged `UNKNOWN_REVIEW_REQUIRED` in the source document and not further resolved here.

## G. Relationship-model analysis

The `PermissionRelationship` graph-of-typed-edges model (pairwise `StackingRelationshipType` rows) generalizes well for the *stacking* question it was designed to answer — every genuinely pairwise interaction found across all five companies (Fixed-basket-plus-ratio-basket additivity, priority-tiered alternative ratio tests, ordered-priority drawdown among 3+ components) maps onto one of the six existing relationship types without needing a seventh. That much of Conclusion 3/4's underlying design holds up honestly, not just because it was proposed first.

It does **not** generalize to the two structural findings that are the headline of this report:

- A **cross-document shared basket cap** (TransDigm, CHS) is not a relationship *between two permissions* at all — it is closer to a single permission whose numeric ceiling is itself computed from a live read of another document's outstanding balance. Modeling it as a `PermissionRelationship` row would require permission A and permission B to live in *different* `Document` rows and the relationship to somehow constrain a *threshold*, not just how the two amounts combine once both are already known — a different kind of edge than anything in the current `StackingRelationshipType` enum, which assumes both permissions are being drawn upon in the *same* transaction, not that one's capacity is defined in terms of the other's *unrelated, independently-accruing* balance.
- A **borrowing-base facility capacity** (Petco ABL, CommScope/Vistance) is not a relationship between permissions at all — it's a single permission whose formula reads external, non-covenant-provision inputs. No pairwise-relationship model, however extended, closes this gap; it needs a new leaf-level `FormulaType`, not a new edge type.

Compared honestly against the alternative of just extending `FormulaType` with more archetypes and leaving relationships out entirely: that alternative fails on the *stacking* questions (Coherent's own §13.1 disregard mechanic, TransDigm's priority-tiered tests, Petco's incremental facility ordering) because those really are properties of *pairs or groups* of permissions, not of any single formula in isolation — a `FormulaType`-only model would have to smuggle relationship information into ad hoc formula parameters, reproducing the graph model badly rather than avoiding it. The relationship-graph model is the right tool for stacking; it is simply not a universal tool for every gap found.

## H. Transaction-requirement analysis

The candidate AND-combined condition taxonomy (`DEBT_PERMISSION` / `LIEN_PERMISSION` / `RATIO_CONDITION` / `PRIORITY_CONDITION` / `GUARANTOR_CONDITION` / `DOCUMENT-SPECIFIC_CONDITION`) holds up well as a **classification scheme for what conditions a transaction has to satisfy**, and every new company supplied at least one confirmed real instance of most of these categories:

- `DEBT_PERMISSION` / `LIEN_PERMISSION` / `RATIO_CONDITION`: confirmed everywhere, unsurprising.
- `PRIORITY_CONDITION`: **directly confirmed and non-trivial** — Petco's Permitted Ratio Debt requires the incurring party's Debt Representative to join the correct Intercreditor Agreement *depending on the lien priority the new debt will have*; this is a real, textual, deterministic AND-branch, not a hypothesis. CHS's automatic pari-passu lien pull-up (§3.6(c)) is a second, structurally different `PRIORITY_CONDITION` instance (a triggered *consequence*, not a precondition).
- `GUARANTOR_CONDITION`: confirmed via the recurring non-guarantor/non-loan-party sub-cap pattern (Petco §7.03(g), TransDigm cl. (14), CHS cl. (11), CommScope/Vistance §6.3(a) proviso) — present in **four of five** companies including Coherent by the task's own scoping choices, making this one of the strongest CORE_CANDIDATE findings in this report.
- `DOCUMENT-SPECIFIC_CONDITION`: confirmed as a real catch-all — TransDigm's "Available Liquidity ≥ $100,000,000" acquisition-eligibility floor and the Investment Grade rating condition gating covenant suspension are each genuine, deterministic, document-specific conditions that don't cleanly belong to any of the other five buckets.

Where it breaks: the taxonomy has **no bucket for a condition on another document's aggregate state** (TransDigm's Notes-outstanding cap living inside the CA's own Permitted Indebtedness; CHS's pooled Credit-Facility-plus-named-Notes cap) and **no bucket for a condition on a company-wide *state* rather than a specific transaction's own facts** (TransDigm's ratings-based covenant suspension, which doesn't test the transaction being proposed at all — it tests whether the *covenant package itself* is currently active). Both are real, AND-combinable conditions in the sense the framework intends, but neither fits cleanly into `DOCUMENT-SPECIFIC_CONDITION` without stretching that bucket to also mean "cross-document state" and "whole-covenant-package state," which would make it do two structurally different jobs under one label.

## I. Dynamic-state analysis

Findings from dimensions 6/7, synthesized: Coherent's step-up/cool-down (a threshold's *value* temporarily changes, triggered by a Material Acquisition, gated by a minimum-consecutive-quarters cooldown before retriggering) turns out to be **one member of a family of at least three structurally distinct dynamic-state mechanics**, not the only shape:

1. **Threshold-value step-up/cool-down** (Coherent only, confirmed in Amendment No. 4) — the covenant is always tested; only the number changes.
2. **Springing/conditional covenant applicability** (Petco ABL, CommScope/Vistance §6.7) — the covenant is tested *at all* only when a liquidity-state gate (excess availability below a floor) is true; the threshold itself never changes. This is a binary ON/OFF switch on *whether a test runs*, not on *what number it's tested against* — genuinely different in kind from (1), even though both are "state-dependent thresholds" in the loose sense dimension 7 uses.
3. **Whole-covenant-package suspension on an external rating trigger, with retroactive re-testing on reversion** (TransDigm Indenture §4.19) — this suspends an entire *list* of covenant sections (not one basket, not one maintenance test), gated on a market-observable fact (rating agency action) rather than anything the company itself does transactionally, and comes with an explicit backward-looking reconciliation rule for what happens to debt incurred during the suspended period.

A fourth mechanic, found only in Coherent's own amendment chain and not corroborated elsewhere in this pass — the opt-out **automatic redesignation** between Incremental Facility components (Cash-Capped → Ratio-Based) and the associated **Reallocated Amount** basket-feeding mechanic — remains `PROVISIONAL_SINGLE_DOCUMENT`; nothing in Petco's, TransDigm's, CHS's, or CommScope/Vistance's read sections showed an analogous *automatic, continuous* reclassification (as opposed to a borrower *election* at the time of incurrence, which is common and already a `CLEAN_MATCH`, per §F).

Every one of these four mechanics classifies differently against the task's own taxonomy: (1) is a **formula parameter** (the threshold value itself becomes a lookup keyed on a state flag); (2) is an **eligibility condition** gating whether a `RATIO_CONDITION` evaluation runs at all; (3) is a **state-transition** at the covenant-package level, closer to a document-wide flag than any single provision's property; (4) is a **relationship** between two specific permissions with an event trigger, not a state on either permission alone. None of them warrant becoming a new `FormulaType`; all four warrant being representable, and none currently are.

## J. Measurement-basis analysis

A generalized, parameterized `CAPACITY_MEASUREMENT_BASIS` concept with `CUMULATIVE_INCURRED` / `CURRENTLY_OUTSTANDING` / `NET_OF_REPAYMENT` / prepayment-history-based as parameter values **suffices for every basket-style permission found in this pass**, including all of Petco's, TransDigm's, and CHS's baskets read. Petco's Fixed Incremental Amount is genuinely new supporting evidence, not just a repeat: it independently confirms the prepayment-history-based basis is a real, recurring market mechanic (now observed in two unrelated real companies, promoting it from Coherent's `PROVISIONAL_SINGLE_DOCUMENT` flag to `CORE_CANDIDATE`), while also showing it doesn't have to be its own separately-ordered basket the way Coherent's is — Petco folds the EBITDA-grower leg and the prepayment-credit leg into **one additive formula**, which is a `DOCUMENT_VARIANT` of how the same measurement basis composes with a `GREATER_OF_FLAT_OR_PCT_EBITDA`-style sibling, not evidence of a fifth basis.

It does **not** suffice for the borrowing-base mechanism (Petco ABL, CommScope/Vistance): a borrowing base isn't measuring *this basket's own history of usage* at all — it measures a live, externally-certified, third-party-verifiable balance-sheet quantity (eligible receivables/inventory) that has no relationship to how much of *this same permission* has previously been drawn. Calling it a fifth `MeasurementBasis` value would misdescribe it in the same way Coherent's own team correctly declined to misdescribe the automatic lien-tied-to-a-basket pattern as a `CONCURRENT_DISREGARDED` relationship (§I.4 of the interaction matrix) — it's a different *kind* of thing, not a variant measurement of the same kind of thing, and belongs in §L as a new `FormulaType` question, not in the `MeasurementBasis` enum.

## K. Permission-graph architecture assessment

Evaluated node-by-node against the five companies' real text:

- **Nodes** (permissions): hold up universally — every company's debt/lien permissions decompose cleanly into `(grants, amountKind, formula, sectionRef)` tuples.
- **Edges** (relationships): hold up for pairwise/grouped stacking (§G) but not for cross-document capacity references (§F/§L) — those need a different edge shape (document-to-document or basket-to-external-balance, not permission-to-permission within one allocation problem) or, more likely, aren't edges at all but a new kind of formula input.
- **State**: Coherent's step-up/cool-down was the only state mechanic the original design anticipated; §I found at least three distinct additional shapes (springing applicability, whole-package suspension, and — Coherent-only — automatic redesignation), meaning "state" needs to be modeled at more than one granularity (basket-level threshold state, covenant-level applicability state, document-level suspension state) rather than as a single mechanism.
- **Transaction**: the AND-combination model (§H) holds up as a classification scheme but needs at least two additional condition categories (cross-document state, whole-covenant-package state) to be complete.
- **Solver objective** (`maxFeasibleAmount` via election enumeration and monotone bisection, §6 of the remediation design): nothing in this pass falsifies the *algorithm* itself — every stacking case found (fixed+ratio additivity, priority-tiered alternatives, ordered 3-way priority) is exactly the shape the proposed allocator was designed to solve. What breaks is upstream of the solver: if a basket's own ceiling depends on another document's live balance, or on an externally-certified collateral figure, the solver's *inputs* aren't fully described by `Permission`/`PermissionRelationship` rows within one document's allocation problem, regardless of how good the solver itself is.

## L. New concepts discovered

Classified per the required taxonomy; each with the company evidence supporting it.

1. **Cross-document/cross-instrument shared basket cap** — one document's Permitted-Debt-style basket ceiling is defined by reference to, or pooled with, another document's or another instrument's outstanding balance. **CORE_CANDIDATE** — observed independently in TransDigm (CA capping Notes balance) and CHS (Notes indenture pooling Credit Facility + named note series under one cap); two unrelated real companies, distinct mechanics within the same family.
2. **Borrowing-base / external-collateral capacity formula** — capacity computed from periodically-certified eligible receivables/inventory advance rates minus lender-discretionary reserves, structurally unrelated to EBITDA or a static dollar figure. **CORE_CANDIDATE** — observed independently in Petco's ABL and CommScope/Vistance's ABL; also a well-known, market-standard leveraged-finance mechanic on its face (asset-based lending is a distinct, textbook facility type), satisfying the taxonomy's "contract-general" prong as well as the two-company prong.
3. **Lender-discretionary reserve/adjustment** — a capacity-reducing input set at counterparty (administrative agent) discretion rather than derived purely from borrower-side financial facts. **CORE_CANDIDATE**, tightly coupled to #2 (same two companies), but flagged separately because it has a distinct legal character (discretionary, not formulaic) that a pure formula representation would hide.
4. **Collateral-pool-scoped priority** — the same permission (or the same instrument) is first-priority security on one defined asset pool and second-priority on a different defined asset pool, simultaneously. **CORE_CANDIDATE** — observed independently in Petco (fixed-asset vs. current-asset collateral split between the First Lien CA and the ABL) and CHS (Non-ABL-Priority vs. ABL-Priority Collateral across three note tranches and the ABL).
5. **Multiple, concurrently-operative intercreditor agreements governing distinct priority relationships** — CHS names three simultaneously (ABL / Senior-Junior / Pari Passu), each governing a different pair or set of creditor classes. **CORE_CANDIDATE** on the general concept "an intercreditor agreement is itself a first-class governing-document type distinct from a credit agreement or indenture, and a company may have more than one" — the specific *number* of agreements (three) is a `LOCAL_CONFIGURATION` detail of CHS, not itself a structural finding.
6. **Intercreditor-agreement joinder as a precondition of a debt-incurrence permission** — a `PRIORITY_CONDITION`-style AND-branch requiring the new debt's representative to become party to a specific, priority-dependent intercreditor agreement before the debt-incurrence permission is available. **CORE_CANDIDATE** — directly textual in Petco's Permitted Ratio Debt clause (b)/(c); structurally implied (though not independently confirmed by reading the agreements themselves) by CHS's entire priority stack.
7. **"Equal and ratable" / negative-pledge lien pull-up** — the inverse of the automatic-lien-tied-to-a-basket pattern: rather than a debt basket bringing its own lien permission, an *unsecured or subordinated-lien* instrument acquires a security interest automatically whenever *other*, more-senior-or-pari debt gets secured. **CORE_CANDIDATE** — observed independently in TransDigm's Indenture §4.12 and CHS's §3.6(c); a well-known, named market mechanic ("equal and ratable security," a standard high-yield/negative-pledge clause) on its face as well.
8. **Springing/conditional covenant applicability keyed to a liquidity-state gate** — a maintenance covenant is tested only when a liquidity measure (excess availability) falls below a floor; the threshold itself never changes. **CORE_CANDIDATE** — observed independently in Petco's ABL and CommScope/Vistance's ABL; also a well-known, textbook ABL mechanic ("springing covenant") on its face.
9. **Whole-covenant-package suspension on an external rating trigger, with retroactive re-testing on reversion** — an entire named list of covenant sections switches off during a Suspension Period gated on Investment Grade Ratings and no continuing Default, and switches back on with an explicit backward-looking reconciliation rule for debt incurred while suspended. **CORE_CANDIDATE** on the general concept (this is one of the most standard, textbook high-yield-indenture mechanics that exists — "covenant fall-away/suspension on investment grade" is a named market convention, satisfying the "contract-general on its face" prong even though it was directly observed in only one of the four comparison companies in this pass).
10. **Good-faith/subjective "no impairment of security interest" covenant** — a covenant whose compliance test is explicitly the issuer's own good-faith determination of whether an action would "materially impair" collateral or lien priority. **LEGAL_JUDGMENT_ONLY** — observed in CHS §3.19; textually and by design not reducible to a deterministic rule.
11. **Nested sub-cap for debt incurred by non-guarantor/non-loan-party subsidiaries, inside a broader basket** — a `GUARANTOR_CONDITION`-flavored parameter that further restricts how much of an otherwise-larger basket may be used by entities outside the guarantor/collateral structure. **CORE_CANDIDATE** — observed independently in Petco, TransDigm, and CHS (three of the four comparison companies), and it is a well-known structural-subordination market mechanic on its face.
12. **"Baseline-freeze" ratio test ("no worse than the level at Closing/immediately prior") used as a permission's *sole* incurrence test, not only as one branch of an explicit OR** — Coherent's leverage-neutral shape (F-3/F-4) was framed as an alternative *within* an `ALT` group; Petco's Permitted Ratio Debt uses the analogous "Closing Date ratio" baseline as the *only* test for Pari Passu/Junior Lien Debt (no numeric-threshold alternative offered at all for that debt type). **DOCUMENT_VARIANT** of primitive #10 in §D, not a new primitive — but the framing that it is always "a specific instance of `ALT`" needs correcting: it can be the sole, non-alternative test.
13. **Liquidity-floor eligibility condition on a transaction (e.g., "Available Liquidity ≥ $X" for an acquisition)** — a `DOCUMENT-SPECIFIC_CONDITION` distinct from a leverage/coverage ratio test. **PROVISIONAL_SINGLE_DOCUMENT** — observed only in TransDigm's Permitted Investments clause 18 in this pass.
14. **Temporary/provisional state for a borrowing-base facility before initial diligence (field exam/appraisal) is complete** — a `Temporary Borrowing Base` stands in for the real formula until specific deliverables are received. **PROVISIONAL_SINGLE_DOCUMENT** — observed only in CommScope/Vistance's ABL in this pass (Petco's ABL text on this specific point was not read closely enough to confirm or rule out an analogous mechanic).
15. **Reporting-frequency obligation keyed to facility utilization** (Monthly vs. Quarterly Reporting Trigger, at a 20%-of-Borrowing-Base threshold) — an operational/reporting mechanic, not a capacity mechanic. **NOT_NEEDED** for the capacity-tracking ontology specifically; flagged only for completeness, since it's adjacent to but distinct from the springing-covenant-applicability concept (#8) it superficially resembles.

## M. Coherent-specific concepts to demote

- **`GREATER_OF_FLAT_OR_PCT_EBITDA`'s implicit assumption that one leg is always a static flat dollar amount.** Petco's general debt basket uses "greater of 50% Closing Date EBITDA and 50% TTM EBITDA" — both legs are EBITDA-percentage-based, with the "flat" character coming only from *which EBITDA snapshot date* is used, not from a hardcoded dollar figure. This still works arithmetically today (both legs are computable numbers), but the `FormulaType`'s name and the mental model it encodes ("a dollar floor, or a percentage of current EBITDA, whichever is greater") is Coherent-drafting-specific phrasing, not a universal shape. **Demote the assumption, not the formula type** — `GREATER_OF_FLAT_OR_PCT_EBITDA` should be understood (and perhaps eventually renamed or re-parameterized) as "greater of two amounts, each independently a flat dollar figure or a percentage of a named EBITDA measure," not "flat-dollar vs. percentage-of-current-EBITDA" specifically.
- **The leverage-neutral test as always framed via `ALT`.** As found in §L.12, Petco uses this test shape as the *sole* incurrence condition for some debt, not one branch of an OR. The underlying ratio-test *logic* (freeze at closing-date level) generalizes fine as a `MATCH_WITH_PARAMETERS` case of an ordinary ratio gate; only the assumption that it always shows up paired with an alternative should be demoted.
- **Framing "Permitted Debt" as necessarily a single named defined term the way Coherent's Indenture uses it.** Both Petco and TransDigm's credit agreements enumerate exceptions directly under a negative covenant section (§7.03/§6.01) without a single umbrella "Permitted Debt" defined term the way Coherent's Indenture and CHS's indenture (via "Permitted Debt" as a catch-all category, §3.2(b)) both do. This is purely a documentary/drafting-convention difference with no substantive effect on the ontology — flagged here only so the eventual data model doesn't assume every document names its exception-clause list the same way; `LOCAL_CONFIGURATION`, not a structural finding.

## O. Minimal proposed core ontology

Only concepts that survive §L/§N's promotion rule, stated with definition, evidence, structural classification, and rationale.

1. **Cross-document capacity reference.** *Definition*: a debt (or, in principle, lien) permission whose numeric ceiling is computed in whole or in part from another document's — or another textually-named instrument's — aggregate outstanding balance, rather than solely from this document's own financial snapshot. *Evidence*: TransDigm CA Permitted Indebtedness cl. (1) ($4.8B Notes cap); CHS Indenture §3.2(b)(1) ($5.011B pooled Credit Facility + named Notes series cap). *Classification*: a new **relationship** type, but one whose two endpoints are a permission and an *external aggregate* (another document's total, or a named cross-document pool identifier) rather than two permissions within the same allocation problem — structurally closer to a constraint than to a `StackingRelationshipType` edge. *Why core*: without it, any cross-document `MIN` combination silently double-counts or over-counts capacity in at least two of four real companies tested; this is not an edge case.
2. **Borrowing-base capacity formula.** *Definition*: a facility's own draw capacity computed as `min(commitment, Σ(advance_rate × eligible_collateral_value) − reserves)`, where eligible collateral values are externally certified/periodic rather than derived from the same financial-snapshot inputs every other `FormulaType` reads. *Evidence*: Petco ABL; CommScope/Vistance ABL. *Classification*: a new **`FormulaType`** (leaf-level calculation), not a relationship or state. *Why core*: it answers a genuinely different question ("how much can be drawn under this specific facility right now") from every other `FormulaType` in the engine ("how much of this basket is available"), and two of four real companies tested have exactly this facility type sitting alongside conventional basket-style debt.
3. **Discretionary capacity adjustment (reserve).** *Definition*: a capacity-reducing input set at the administrative agent's/lender's discretion rather than computed purely from the borrower's own reported facts. *Evidence*: Petco ABL "Reserves"; CommScope/Vistance ABL "Reserves." *Classification*: an **attribute** of the borrowing-base `FormulaType` (item 2), not a freestanding primitive — it doesn't recur outside that formula shape in the documents read. *Why core*: omitting it makes a borrowing-base capacity figure systematically overstated versus what a lender will actually advance; it's not cosmetic.
4. **Collateral pool.** *Definition*: a named, defined asset grouping (e.g., "ABL-Priority Collateral," "Non-ABL Priority Collateral," "fixed asset collateral," "current asset collateral") against which lien priority is separately assigned; a single lien permission or instrument can be first-priority on one pool and second-priority on another simultaneously. *Evidence*: Petco (First Lien CA vs. ABL split); CHS (three-way ABL/Senior/Junior split across two named pools). *Classification*: a new **entity/attribute** — lien priority becomes a property of a `(permission, collateral pool)` pair, not a single scalar on the permission. *Why core*: without it, "priority" has nowhere to attach when the same instrument is simultaneously senior on one pool and junior on another — a single first/second-lien flag per permission cannot represent this and is affirmatively wrong for two of four real companies tested.
5. **Intercreditor agreement (as a governing-document type).** *Definition*: a document distinct from a credit agreement or indenture that governs relative rights, enforcement control, and standstill/payment-priority among multiple classes of secured creditors; a company may have more than one, each governing a different pair or set of classes. *Evidence*: Petco (ABL Intercreditor Agreement, referenced); CHS (three named agreements). *Classification*: a new **`DocumentType`** enum value (alongside `CREDIT_AGREEMENT`/`INDENTURE`/`OTHER`), plus a **relationship** from a debt/lien permission to the specific intercreditor agreement(s) it is subject to. *Why core*: it recurs, is textually load-bearing (permissions explicitly condition on joinder — see item 6), and `OTHER` currently has nowhere to hang the "which specific intercreditor agreement" fact even as a stopgap.
6. **Intercreditor joinder as a transaction precondition.** *Definition*: a debt-incurrence permission's own conditions include "the incurring party's representative must become (or already be) a party to intercreditor agreement X," where X depends on the new debt's intended priority. *Evidence*: Petco Permitted Ratio Debt (b)/(c). *Classification*: a new **`PRIORITY_CONDITION`**-category transaction condition, AND-combined alongside `RATIO_CONDITION`/`GUARANTOR_CONDITION` per the existing dimension-H framework, referencing item 5. *Why core*: it is a real, deterministic, textually-confirmed gate on whether a transaction clears — omitting it means a transaction could be reported "clear" on ratio grounds alone while failing a real contractual precondition.
7. **Equal-and-ratable lien pull-up.** *Definition*: an instrument automatically acquires (or must be granted) security equal and ratable with — or senior to, if subordinated — any other debt that becomes secured, without needing to itself qualify under a named debt basket first. *Evidence*: TransDigm Indenture §4.12; CHS §3.6(c). *Classification*: a **relationship**, structurally the inverse of the existing automatic-lien-tied-to-a-basket primitive (candidate #8 in §D) — same family, opposite direction of dependency. *Why core*: it is a named, standard market mechanic and was independently confirmed in two real companies; representing it by reusing the automatic-lien-tied primitive backwards would misdescribe which side is driving which.
8. **Springing/conditional covenant applicability.** *Definition*: a maintenance covenant is tested only while a specified liquidity (or other state) condition holds; the covenant's own threshold never changes. *Evidence*: Petco ABL; CommScope/Vistance §6.7. *Classification*: an **eligibility condition** / gating attribute on a `RATIO_CONDITION`-type maintenance covenant, structurally distinct from a `CovenantProvision`'s `thresholdValue`. *Why core*: it changes whether a covenant result is even relevant to a given evaluation date, which affects `EvaluationStatus` reporting (a covenant that is currently untested-because-inapplicable must not be silently reported as `not_tested`-meaning-unknown, nor as passing).
9. **Covenant-package suspension state.** *Definition*: an entire named list of covenant sections is suspended while an external condition (e.g., Investment Grade Ratings + no continuing Default) holds, with an explicit rule for retroactively re-testing debt incurred during the suspension once the condition lapses. *Evidence*: TransDigm Indenture §4.19 (market-standard on its face). *Classification*: a document-level (or covenant-group-level) **state** field, with an associated **event** (Reversion Date) and **calculation** (retroactive re-test against the reinstated covenant). *Why core*: this is one of the most common real-world high-yield mechanics; a company-agnostic engine that cannot represent it at all would be structurally unable to model a large fraction of actual investment-grade-trending high-yield issuers correctly, not just Coherent's peer set.
10. **Nested sub-cap for non-guarantor-incurred debt.** *Definition*: within a broader basket, a tighter secondary ceiling applies specifically to the portion incurred by non-guarantor/non-loan-party restricted subsidiaries. *Evidence*: Petco §7.03(g); TransDigm cl. (14); CHS cl. (11). *Classification*: a **parameter** on the relevant `FormulaType` (an optional secondary threshold + a `GUARANTOR_CONDITION` scope), not a new relationship or state. *Why core*: recurs in a majority of the companies tested; a basket's stated dollar figure is not the whole story of what a non-guarantor subsidiary can actually draw against it.

## P. Configurable contract-specific layer

The following vary by document and belong as parameters on the existing (or the ten newly-proposed) primitives, not as new types:

- All specific dollar amounts, percentages, and ratio thresholds (obviously, and already the design's own stance).
- Which EBITDA measurement date/definition anchors a "greater of" basket's percentage leg (Closing Date EBITDA vs. TTM EBITDA vs. Four Quarter Consolidated EBITDA, etc.) — a parameter on the `FormulaType`, per §M.
- The specific number and names of intercreditor agreements a company has (one, per Petco; three, per CHS) — a cardinality/parameter detail of the new `DocumentType`/relationship pair (item 5/6 in §O), not a structural difference.
- Which specific covenant sections are on a suspension list (item 9 in §O) — a per-document list, not a fixed enumeration.
- The specific liquidity-state formula gating a springing covenant (greater of a flat dollar figure and a percentage of commitments/borrowing base, in both companies observed, but the exact percentage and floor vary) — a parameter.
- Advance rates, NOLV percentages, and eligibility criteria inside a borrowing-base formula — parameters on the new borrowing-base `FormulaType` (item 2 in §O).
- Whether a document names its exception-clause list "Permitted Debt" as a single defined term or enumerates it directly under a negative covenant section (§M) — purely presentational, a citation/labeling detail, not a capacity-modeling one.

## Q. Human-judgment boundary

Confirmed, and one addition beyond what Coherent's own documents exposed:

- **Covenant applicability to a specific transaction path** (Coherent Conclusion 5) — reconfirmed as a live issue in every company; e.g., whether CHS's §3.2(b)(4) grandfathering language reaches a specific refinancing structure is a drafting-interpretation question, not a formula.
- **Whether multiple interpretations of an ambiguous cross-reference remain genuinely open** (Coherent's "§6.11(i)" saga) — this pattern recurs qualitatively (TransDigm's own Permitted Indebtedness text was read without independently confirming every cross-reference resolves cleanly, per the SOURCE_CHAIN_INCOMPLETE notes in §C) and remains a permanent, structural category of judgment call, not something a future data model closes off.
- **New addition: good-faith/subjective standards embedded directly in covenant text** (CHS §3.19's "might reasonably or would (in the good faith determination of the Issuer) have the result of materially impairing" the security interests) — this is qualitatively different from an interpretive ambiguity about what a defined term means; it is a covenant whose *compliance test itself* is explicitly subjective by the document's own drafting, not merely difficult to interpret. No amount of better modeling collapses this into a deterministic rule; it belongs permanently in this bucket, and the engine's fail-closed design (never silently resolving `review_required` into `clear`) is the correct posture for it, not a gap to be closed.

## R. Ontology stability metrics

A rough diagnostic, not a rigorous metric — stated plainly per instruction.

- **Candidate concepts entering the test** (§D, items 1–16, counting the 6-way `StackingRelationshipType` enum as one concept and its members as sub-cases): **16**.
- **Observed in 2+ of the 5 structures** (Coherent counted as one): grant-type split, amount-kind split, automatic lien tie, stacking-relationship taxonomy (as a category), priority-tiered ratio test, reclassification election, `ALT`/best-path-wins semantics, measurement-basis family (extended) — **8 of 16** confirmed as genuinely cross-company-general.
- **Observed in 3+ structures**: grant-type split, amount-kind split, automatic lien tie, reclassification election — **4 of 16**.
- **Need-only-parameterization** (i.e., `MATCH_WITH_PARAMETERS`/`CLEAN_MATCH` outcomes throughout §E–§F): the same **8** counted above, plus the nested-guarantor-sub-cap pattern once it is folded in as a parameter rather than a new type (see §O item 10) — **9 of 16** candidate concepts, plus new concept L.11.
- **Genuinely new** (§L items classified `CORE_CANDIDATE`, i.e., promoted to §O): **10** (cross-document capacity reference, borrowing-base formula, discretionary reserve, collateral pool, intercreditor-agreement document type, intercreditor-joinder condition, equal-and-ratable pull-up, springing covenant applicability, covenant-package suspension state, nested guarantor sub-cap).
- **Coherent-only, not promoted** (remains `PROVISIONAL_SINGLE_DOCUMENT` after this pass): automatic opt-out redesignation between basket components; the Reallocated Amount basket-feeding mechanic; the Priority Debt/Collateral Suspension Period concept (already flagged `UNKNOWN_REVIEW_REQUIRED` in the source document itself) — **3 concepts**, all from Coherent's own Amendment No. 4, none corroborated elsewhere in this pass.
- **Better modeled as a relationship vs. a state-transition**, among the newly-discovered concepts: relationship-shaped — cross-document capacity reference, equal-and-ratable pull-up, intercreditor joinder (3); state-transition-shaped — springing covenant applicability, covenant-package suspension (2); leaf-formula-shaped — borrowing-base formula, discretionary reserve (2); entity/attribute-shaped — collateral pool, intercreditor-agreement document type (2); parameter-shaped — nested guarantor sub-cap (1).
- **Human-judgment-only**: good-faith impairment-of-security covenant (newly found, §Q) plus the two already-established Coherent categories (covenant applicability to a path; genuine cross-reference ambiguity) — **3** (1 new, 2 reconfirmed).

**Ontology reuse ratio** = (reused generalized concepts) / (total materially distinct concepts encountered across all 5 companies). Using the counts above: 9 reused (need-only-parameterization) out of a total of 9 (reused) + 10 (genuinely new) + 3 (Coherent-only, not promoted) = **22** materially distinct concepts encountered → **9/22 ≈ 0.41**. Restated plainly: this is a rough diagnostic built from this session's own classification judgment calls on a non-exhaustive reading of four companies' documents — it is not a statistically rigorous figure, and a different reviewer reading different sections of the same documents could reasonably land on a different count in either direction. Its only defensible use is directional: under half of what Coherent's design proposed survived this pass unchanged, and the majority of what didn't survive is not noise — it clusters into a small number of coherent, re-observed structural gaps (§L items 1–11), which is a stronger signal than the raw ratio alone.

## S. Implications for future solver

Capabilities only — not a design.

- The solver must be able to accept, as an input to a single basket's ceiling computation, **a live read of another document's (or another named instrument's) current aggregate outstanding balance**, not just this document's own `CovenantProvision`/`FinancialSnapshot` rows — confirmed necessary by two of four real companies tested.
- The solver must be able to evaluate **at least one `FormulaType` whose inputs are not derived from `FinancialSnapshot`/`CovenantProvision` at all**, but from a separately-tracked, periodically-updated eligible-collateral figure with an explicit discretionary-adjustment component.
- The solver's notion of "priority" must be resolvable **per collateral pool**, not per permission — a single global secured/unsecured or first/second-lien flag on a permission is insufficient for at least two of four real companies tested; asking "what is this instrument's priority" needs an answer that can differ by which asset pool is being asked about.
- The solver must be able to represent, and factor into whether a transaction is `clear`, **a precondition that references a separate governing document (an intercreditor agreement) that is neither the credit agreement nor the indenture being evaluated** — and must be able to distinguish "this precondition is unmet" from "this precondition's status was not investigated" (the latter being the correct status for most of what this report marks `SOURCE_CHAIN_INCOMPLETE`, since the intercreditor agreements' own text was never read).
- The solver must support **evaluating a covenant's applicability itself as a gated, potentially time-varying fact** — separately from evaluating the covenant's result once applicable — for both the springing-covenant shape (liquidity-gated, single covenant) and the suspension-on-rating-trigger shape (external-event-gated, whole covenant-package, with a defined retroactive reconciliation rule for the reversion date).
- The solver must be able to report a result as `LEGAL_JUDGMENT_ONLY`/`review_required` for a covenant whose own compliance test is explicitly framed as the issuer's good-faith, subjective determination — this is a fail-closed requirement, not an enhancement, and follows directly from the engine's existing design philosophy rather than requiring new philosophy.
- The solver must be able to represent a **nested, narrower sub-limit that applies only to the portion of a basket's usage attributable to a specific class of obligor** (non-guarantor/non-loan-party subsidiaries), as a constraint on the *allocation within* a basket, not merely on the basket's own headline ceiling.

## T. Recommended next step

**Do another round of document-diversity stress testing — specifically targeting the two structural gaps this report could not fully resolve on the evidence in hand: (1) reading the intercreditor agreements themselves (not just what an indenture says about them) at Community Health Systems or a comparably intercreditor-heavy company, to confirm whether standstill/payment-blockage/enforcement-control provisions introduce transaction-condition shapes beyond the `PRIORITY_CONDITION` joinder-precondition pattern already confirmed; and (2) reading a second company's full incremental-facility/MFN (most-favored-nation) mechanics in complete, non-restated form, since TransDigm's own numeric Incremental Facility formula was marked `SOURCE_CHAIN_INCOMPLETE` in this pass and Petco's was the only fully-read example of that mechanic family.**

This is the right call, not the more convenient "proceed to design the solver," for three reasons grounded in this report's own evidence rather than in caution for its own sake. First, ten new `CORE_CANDIDATE` primitives (§O) is a large number to freeze an architecture around after reading only four companies' documents non-exhaustively — the discovery rate did not visibly taper off across the four companies (each contributed at least one genuinely new, unrepeated-elsewhere finding: TransDigm's covenant suspension, CHS's three-agreement intercreditor stack and pooled cross-series cap, CommScope/Vistance's temporary-borrowing-base state, Petco's intercreditor-joinder precondition), which is itself evidence that the space of real-market mechanics is not yet saturated by this sample. Second, two specific, identified evidentiary gaps (the intercreditor agreements' own text; a complete incremental-facility formula) sit directly underneath two of the ten proposed core primitives (items 5/6 and none directly, though item 1's TransDigm evidence is also `SOURCE_CHAIN_INCOMPLETE` on the exact numeric formula) — closing them is a bounded, specific piece of work, not an open-ended fishing expedition, and doing it before freezing a solver design is cheaper than discovering the gap after the solver is built around an incomplete input model. Third, and most importantly: this report already found that a purely relationship-graph solver (§G/§K) is *not* sufficient on its own — it needs a genuinely new `FormulaType` (borrowing-base) and a genuinely new entity (collateral pool) that the original remediation design's §6 allocation algorithm never anticipated as inputs. Designing that solver now, before confirming (via one more round of real documents) that this report has actually found the complete input surface it needs to handle, risks building the algorithm around an ontology that a fifth or sixth company could still falsify in the same way this round falsified parts of the Coherent-only baseline.
