# Inspiration

Read on 17 October 2026 from public pages only. Nothing here is a brand to copy. No logos, no palettes, no slogans moved into the product.

Sources that actually loaded: Ramp, Brex, Mercury, Stripe, Carta, Rippling, Pigment, Linear’s marketing site, Linear’s own redesign essay (28 March 2024), a published audit of Linear empty states, Vercel, Notion, Apple’s homepage, Harvey, Ironclad, 9fin, and Octus (the company that absorbed Covenant Review). Bloomberg’s professional page timed out, so it is not used as evidence.

The CFO’s question on Headroom is one sentence: how much headroom can we rely on, and what document makes that true or not.

## Ramp

Source: https://ramp.com

The page opens with four words and a promise, then a single platform claim. Proof is a named controller or CFO, not a feature grid. A quote from a fractional CFO sits next to the work, not in a footer.

Adopt: open on the decision, and put a named person’s sentence next to it only when we have one.

Avoid: the celebrity stunt, the “$1M+ saved” tile with no definition, and five products presented as equal.

## Brex

Source: https://www.brex.com

“Finance built for speed and control” is a pair, not a paragraph. The metric row carries a date: internal figures from March 2025, and a line that past performance does not guarantee results.

Adopt: if a number is shown, say what it measures and when it was measured.

Avoid: a row of equal product tiles (cards, expenses, travel, bills, banking) before the number the buyer came for.

## Mercury

Source: https://mercury.com

The promise is that banking tasks take seconds, and that available funds are one snapshot. Search jumps to a payment or a transaction. A founder of Linear is quoted on doing the work inside the app in a minute or two.

Adopt: one snapshot of what is available, and search as the way to the rest.

Avoid: yield, cashback, and “radically different” as the voice of a covenant product.

## Stripe

Source: https://stripe.com

The marketing page still shows a small product module: plan name, billing cadence, a price, and usage in the last 30 days. Large figures (“$1.9T”, “99.999%”) sit with a unit and a year. The rest of the page is infrastructure, not a dashboard of charts.

Adopt: one module that is a label, a figure, and the period. Supporting facts stay smaller than the figure.

Avoid: treating the logo wall, the uptime claim, and the product module as the same size.

## Carta

Source: https://carta.com

Customers describe one set of books used for administration and tax, and a cap table that updates within a day or two of a capital event. The CFO quote is about reviewing a high volume of data in less time because it is the same data.

Adopt: one figure, and the sentence that says which record it came from.

Avoid: leading with an AI plugin, and a wall of product names before the cap table.

## Rippling

Source: https://www.rippling.com

The pitch is one record of the company, so a report that used to take 90 minutes is a prompt. A CFO is quoted on fully burdened cost, not on software.

Adopt: the next action is one report or one setup step, not a tour of modules.

Avoid: HR, IT, devices, and finance on the same first screen. Headroom is not a suite.

## Pigment

Source: https://www.pigment.com

The line that matters is “designed for decisions that can’t wait.” A CFO describes the job as circulating information, diagnosing, and presenting a solution. Frames are described as the gap between the model and the executive view. Drivers and assumptions are the product, not a chart gallery.

Adopt: the executive view is the decision. The model, the drivers, and the history stay behind it until they are loaded.

Avoid: an agent gallery, a frame gallery, and a planning slogan above an empty grid.

## Linear

Sources: https://linear.app, https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui, and the empty-state audit at https://www.northbase.design/systems/linear/empty-states

The marketing site shows one issue, its activity, and a diff. The redesign essay is more useful than the homepage. They reduced chrome, aligned the sidebar and tabs until the alignment is felt rather than seen, and put meta properties in a side panel so the main view stays a list, a board, or a document. Accent color is limited. Text contrast was raised. Headings use a display cut of the same family as the body.

The empty-state audit of 11 Linear screens: a search with no hits is a sentence, not an illustration. A first-use project can teach, with one action. They do not illustrate every empty widget on a dashboard.

Adopt: a side panel for the row you already have. An empty chart is a sentence, not a frame. One accent, used for the action and the focus ring.

Avoid: 32-pixel rows of learned icons. A CFO reads the covenant name. Also avoid a shimmering skeleton that is more animated than the answer.

## Vercel

Source: https://vercel.com

Two actions: deploy, or talk to sales. Feature lines are short enough to scan. The page does not explain the company before the action.

Adopt: one primary action on a view. A second action is quiet.

Avoid: “agentic infrastructure” as a tone, and a feature list that repeats itself.

## Notion

Source: https://www.notion.com

The useful line is “get answers, instantly — with citations.” The rest is a workspace that tries to hold every kind of work.

Adopt: an answer that points at its source.

Avoid: a blank page that invites you to build the tool, and a home that is a pile of databases.

## Apple

Source: https://www.apple.com

The homepage is a stack of single products. Each one is a name and a few words (“Pro further.”, “A battery you can’t outrun.”). There is no dashboard.

Adopt: the first screen is one object and a few words. Scale the type until the answer is the page.

Avoid: launch theater, whimsy, and a second product competing with the first.

## Harvey

Source: https://www.harvey.ai

The audience is named: law firms and in-house teams, highest-stakes work. Security is a section (SSO, audit logs, allow lists), not a row of badges. A general counsel is quoted on strategic value, not on the model.

Adopt: say who the screen is for, and keep the control story in one place.

Avoid: “frontier” language, and a platform diagram before the matter.

## Ironclad

Source: https://ironcladapp.com

The quote that belongs in this product: when contract data is structured, a question takes minutes, not days. The object is the contract. The page also showed metric tiles whose numbers did not come through (“% reduction”), which is a broken promise.

Adopt: the clause is the object. A metric tile without its number is worse than no tile.

Avoid: four percentage tiles, analyst-quadrant badges, and “all things contracts” as a headline.

## 9fin

Source: https://www.9fin.com

Built for credit teams. The line to keep: move from question to decision, and every output is cited and traceable. Coverage is split by the job (origination, restructuring, private credit), not by a generic dashboard.

Adopt: the path is question, figure, source. If the source is missing, say so in the same place as the figure.

Avoid: “AI-native” as the headline, and a coverage map of every credit market on the company page.

## Octus

Source: https://www.octus.com

Octus is the public home of the credit franchise that includes Covenant Review. The promise is that credit becomes clear because the library is expert-checked and the documents are searchable. News items carry a date. The page then lists FinDox, Sky Road, LevPro, and a stack of AI connectors.

Adopt: a dated source under a credit conclusion. The document is how you trust the number.

Avoid: a product-name pile, and a mobile-app pitch on the decision screen.

## Bloomberg

The professional site did not load. No pattern is taken from it. The credit tools that did load (9fin, Octus) already cover the terminal lesson: a dated, sourced row beats a decorated chart.

## What Headroom should take

These are the patterns worth building. They are the behavior, not the brand.

1. One answer, set in large type, with the unit and the caveat in a smaller line. From Apple’s single-product pages, Mercury’s one snapshot, and Stripe’s plan module.
2. An empty measure is a sentence in the place the chart would have been. From Linear’s empty states and from Ironclad’s broken percentage tiles.
3. The source sits under the figure or the clause. From 9fin, Octus, Notion’s citation line, and Carta’s “same data.”
4. Everything that is not the answer is a quieter row, or a side panel of fields the page already has. From Linear’s side panels and the redesign’s reduced chrome.
5. One primary action. A second action is a quiet link. From Vercel’s two-button page and Rippling’s one next report.
6. A number is shown only with what it measures. Unknown stays a sentence. From Brex’s dated footnote and from Headroom’s own honesty rule.
7. Search reaches the rest of the product so the first screen does not have to. From Mercury’s universal search. Headroom already has Jump to.
8. Do not teach a CFO an icon language. The covenant is words. From the Linear density we are refusing, and from Ironclad’s “the contract is the object.”
